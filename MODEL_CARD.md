# Model Card — Pratyaya Credit Risk Model

Following the model card framework of Mitchell et al. (2019). Every figure here
is regenerated by `python scripts/train.py`, `python eval/run_eval.py`,
`python scripts/validate_on_real_data.py` and
`python scripts/compare_mitigations.py`, and written to `eval/reports/`.

---

## Model details

| | |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Estimate probability of default within 12 months for a retail credit application, for thin-file and new-to-credit applicants in India |
| **Type** | `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` (scikit-learn), wrapped in `CalibratedClassifierCV` with isotonic regression |
| **Version** | 0.1.0 |
| **Inputs** | 24 features across application details, credit bureau record, consented Account Aggregator banking/UPI behaviour, and telecom/utility punctuality |
| **Output** | Calibrated probability of default in [0, 1], plus a decision against a policy threshold |
| **Training regime** | Three-way split: 60% fit, 15% calibration, 25% held-out test. The booster never sees the calibration split; calibration never sees the test split |
| **Licence** | Prototype, not licensed for production use |

**Why this estimator.** Two properties decided it. It handles `NaN` natively,
which matters because for a new-to-credit applicant a missing bureau score is
not a data-quality defect to impute away — it is the defining fact about them.
And it has no compiled system dependency: LightGBM and XGBoost both dynamically
link `libomp` on macOS, which breaks installation on any machine without
Homebrew, including a reviewer's.

---

## Intended use

**In scope.** Assisting a human underwriting decision for small-ticket
unsecured retail credit, where the applicant has consented to share financial
data through the Account Aggregator framework. The model produces a score, five
reason codes, and actionable recourse; a person remains accountable for the
decision.

**Out of scope.**

- Fully automated adverse action with no route to human review.
- Any population unlike the training population — secured lending, corporate
  credit, or markets outside India.
- Collections, pricing, or credit-limit management. The model was trained to
  rank origination risk and was never validated for those.
- Any use of the score as a general measure of a person's character or
  trustworthiness.

---

## Factors

**Evaluated groups.** Gender (female/male, as recorded), region tier
(metro/tier-2/tier-3/rural), and age band (21-30/31-40/41-50/51+).

**These are never model inputs.** Protected attributes are excluded
structurally in `feature_columns()` rather than dropped downstream, and a test
asserts they cannot appear in a reason code. They exist in the dataset only so
that fairness can be measured.

**Not evaluated.** Caste, religion, disability and marital status are neither
collected nor generated, so disparities along those axes are unmeasured and
unknown. This is a real limitation, not an assurance.

## Metrics

| Metric | Value | Why this metric |
|---|---|---|
| ROC AUC | 0.7797 | Ranking quality |
| Gini | 0.5594 | The same, in the form credit teams quote |
| KS statistic | 0.4127 | The separation measure a risk committee expects |
| Brier score | 0.0922 | Accuracy of the probability itself |
| Expected calibration error | 0.0073 | Whether a stated 8% risk means 8 in 100 |
| Bad rate among approved | 0.0623 | The number the lending book actually feels |

**Decision threshold.** Set by approval rate rather than by an abstract
probability: the cutoff is the quantile of predicted risk that approves 70% of
applicants, which is how lending policy is actually expressed. At the current
model that is a probability of default of 0.1586.

## Training data

**The population is synthetic.** No public dataset joins Account
Aggregator-style alternative data to realised loan outcomes for Indian
borrowers; that data is personal financial information and is not published.
The generator is documented in `ml/data/generator.py`.

- 30,000 applicants, 12.0% default rate, 59% new-to-credit.
- Default is generated from two latent variables — repayment capacity and
  willingness — plus the true instalment burden and a large idiosyncratic shock.
  Observable features are noisy consequences of those latents, never rescaled
  copies of the label. Maximum feature-to-label correlation is 0.49.
- **Two biases are deliberately seeded**: bureau records go missing more often
  for women (70.6% vs 51.0% new-to-credit) and rural applicants (67.5% vs
  51.8%), and declared income is depressed for women. Neither affects true
  repayment, so true default rates are equal across groups to within 0.4
  percentage points. Any approval gap the model produces is therefore
  unjustified by risk — which is what makes the fairness result measurable.

**Signals deliberately not collected.** Social graphs, contact lists, SMS
content, app-usage timing, and caste or religion proxies are not generated,
stored or modelled. Some are predictive. The RBI Digital Lending Directions
restrict collection to what is need-based and the DPDP Act 2023 requires purpose
limitation; excluding them at source is a stronger guarantee than collecting
them and promising restraint.

## Evaluation data

Two independent evaluations:

**1. Held-out split** of the synthetic population (7,500 applicants), never seen
during fitting or calibration.

**2. Two real public credit datasets**, run through the identical code path, to
show the machinery is not an artefact of the generator:

| Dataset | Rows | AUC | KS | ECE | TreeSHAP additivity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| German Credit (Statlog) | 1,000 | 0.7464 | 0.3752 | 0.0772 | 2.7e-15 |
| Taiwan Default of Credit Card Clients | 30,000 | 0.7829 | 0.4391 | 0.0222 | 4.0e-15 |
| *This model, synthetic* | 30,000 | 0.7797 | 0.4127 | 0.0073 | 5.2e-15 |

The synthetic population's difficulty sits between the two real datasets, which
is the answer to the objection that the generator was tuned to a flattering
level. German Credit's higher calibration error is honest: 1,000 rows leaves a
150-row calibration split.

**What the real datasets do not establish.** Neither contains alternative data,
so the traditional-versus-inclusive comparison below cannot be reproduced on
them. That finding rests on the synthetic population.

## Quantitative analyses

### The inclusion result

Two models, identical data and splits, approval rate pinned at 70%.

| | Bureau only | + Alternative data |
|---|---|---|
| ROC AUC | 0.7314 | 0.7797 |
| Bad rate among approved | 0.0746 | 0.0623 |
| Disparate impact (gender) | 0.869 | 0.964 |
| **Qualified-approval gap** | **0.1083** | **0.0341** |

Among applicants who **would have repaid**: 70.9% of women were approved
against 81.7% of men under bureau-only underwriting — a 10.8-point penalty on
people who would have paid the loan back. Adding alternative data narrows that
to 3.4 points while raising AUC and lowering the bad rate.

### Compared against algorithmic mitigation

| Strategy | AUC | Bad rate | DI | Needs attribute at decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | 0.731 | 0.0698 | 0.879 | no |
| **Alternative data** | **0.765** | **0.0616** | 0.957 | **no** |
| CorrelationRemover | 0.727 | 0.0708 | 0.990 | **yes** |
| ThresholdOptimizer | 0.677 | 0.1200 | 1.000 | **yes** |
| ExponentiatedGradient | *degenerate* | 0.1191 | 0.996 | no |

Every algorithmic remedy bought fairness with accuracy or with risk. Widening
the evidence was the only intervention that improved discrimination, risk and
fairness together.

Two failures worth recording. `CorrelationRemover` cannot accept `NaN`, so the
missing bureau records had to be imputed before it would run — in thin-file
lending that absence is precisely the signal, and the method cannot see it.
`ExponentiatedGradient` collapsed to approving ~99% of applicants across five
constraint tightnesses (eps 0.02 to 0.40) and all three constraint types tried
(demographic parity, equalized odds, true-positive-rate parity). With a 12% base
rate, approving everyone satisfies parity exactly while being only 12% wrong,
and the reduction finds that optimum. It emits two distinct scores, so it cannot
be operated at a chosen approval rate. This is a known failure mode of
reductions on imbalanced outcomes rather than evidence the method is unsound;
making it usable here would need class rebalancing or a cost-sensitive
objective, which was out of scope.

### Explanation faithfulness

TreeSHAP additivity error is 5.2e-15 across 500 applicants — the reason codes
sum exactly to the model's output. An earlier version splitting on categoricals
natively produced errors up to 0.24 in log-odds, enough to reorder an
applicant's reason codes. Under the RBI Fair Practices Code the lender must
convey the *actual* reason for rejection, so an unfaithful explanation is a
compliance failure rather than a cosmetic one. The property is asserted in the
test suite.

## Ethical considerations

**Mitigation must not require the protected attribute at decision time.**
Fairlearn's `ThresholdOptimizer` achieves parity by applying a different
threshold per group. Statistically effective; in lending it means an applicant's
gender changes the bar they must clear, which is disparate treatment rather than
a remedy for it. Two applicants with identical financial records receive an
identical decision from this model.

**The language model runs live and is measured.** Ten of ten consecutive
requests narrated successfully on `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` at 2.8 s mean. On an
earlier model choice, five of ten fell back to the deterministic notice under
rate limiting, and all five still returned the correct decision, reasons,
recourse and citations.

**The language model cannot change a decision.** A deterministic model decides;
the LLM only narrates, and its output is discarded if it contradicts the
decision. A hallucination can produce a badly worded sentence, never a wrong
outcome.

**No personal data reaches a third-party model.** The prompt is assembled only
from the decision object, never the applicant record. There is no code path by
which a name, PAN, Aadhaar or phone number could be sent to a provider, and the
API rejects requests carrying such fields with a 422.

**Recourse is constrained to be honest.** Only factors an applicant can move are
offered — never age, education or employment sector. Targets are capped at the
85th percentile of the observed population. When no attainable change would flip
the decision, that is reported rather than padded with an impossible suggestion.

## Caveats and recommendations

1. **Do not deploy this model.** It is trained on a synthetic population. The
   architecture, the explainability and the audit are transferable; the fitted
   parameters are not.
2. **Reject inference is unhandled.** Repayment is only observed for applicants
   past policy approved, so any real training population is censored by prior
   decisions. This is the largest methodological gap.
3. **Alternative-data features drift faster than bureau data.** UPI behaviour can
   shift within a quarter. Monitoring would need to be more frequent than a
   traditional scorecard's.
4. **The guardrail evaluation is self-authored.** F1 of 1.000 over 23 cases shows
   the guardrails catch the attacks anticipated, not that they catch all attacks.
5. **Fairness is measured on three attributes only.** Caste, religion, disability
   and marital status are unmeasured.
6. **The regulatory corpus is paraphrased**, not verbatim law, and is labelled as
   such throughout.
